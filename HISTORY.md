# How the framing evolved

The catalogue now starts with everyday human outcomes: abundance, peace and room for happiness.

The earlier set focused too heavily on institutional workflows. PWS-0101–PWS-0124 are now marked archived in place so existing links remain readable. They are not part of the current catalogue. Some may serve as technical components underneath a human-outcome brief.

The original broad map and previous catalogue remain in [the previous revision](https://github.com/futurelabstudios/problems-worth-solving/tree/080aa1111c21a8f0130383668cce7d979cd879b3).

Earlier IDs PWS-0001–PWS-0005 were withdrawn; PWS-0006–PWS-0010 were mentioned but never published as briefs. IDs through PWS-0100 remain reserved. PWS-0101–PWS-0124 preserve the former technical drafts. The current 24 briefs are PWS-0201–PWS-0224; new contributions should start after PWS-0224.

## What changed in the standard

The deciding question is now whether people living with a problem would value its resolution. Technical progress must demonstrably reach them through price, access, health, time, security, relationships or agency.

The illustrative voices are authored hypotheses. Affected-person review is required before calling the framing validated.
