# A future worth building, and questions we can work on

This is an invitation to extend the project's ambitions. It is not a forecast, an additional problem catalogue, or a claim that the technologies below are achievable. The [everyday problems](CATALOGUE.md) remain the starting point for measurable human benefit.

An ordinary day could contain much more freedom: the means to leave unwanted work, a body that supports the life we choose, useful things within reach, and time to explore, create and celebrate. Removing today's obstacles matters in its own right. It can also teach us which institutions and capabilities would make a more ambitious future worth living in.

## Five ambitions with work underneath them

### Freedom to choose a life

Economic freedom includes access to productive resources and the ability to refuse harmful work without losing essentials. Freedom of conscience includes spiritual practice, questioning, changing one's beliefs, or choosing none. No institution or AI should define a single correct form of fulfillment.

The current briefs on [dependable work](problems/security/PWS-0207.md), [financial shocks](problems/security/PWS-0208.md) and [time lost to chores](problems/time/PWS-0210.md) address parts of this ambition. A further question is who receives the gains from automation. Compare pay, hours, ownership and shared-income arrangements after costs. Ask whether a person can actually leave, decline or choose differently. More production alone does not answer that question; control of resources and allocation rules belong in the explanation.

### Material abundance within reach

Imagine reliable clean energy and the ability to make, repair and recover useful things without scarcity dominating everyday decisions. The replicator is a fictional expression of this hope. “Infinite energy” and unrestricted editing of atoms describe an aspiration, not engineering specifications.

[Affordable housing](problems/essentials/PWS-0201.md), [food](problems/essentials/PWS-0202.md) and [household energy](problems/essentials/PWS-0203.md) supply existing tests of whether capability reaches people. A practical research question is when local fabrication makes a durable replacement part cheaper and easier to obtain than purchasing it. Count design, failed attempts, energy, equipment and delivery. At a larger scale, ask which constraints lie in generation, grids, feedstock, manufacturing, ownership or access.

### More healthy life, on our own terms

The ambition is to defeat disease, aging-related loss of function and ultimately involuntary death, while preserving each person's choices about care and life. This is an aspiration, not a prediction of immortality or an obligation to seek life extension.

The briefs on [appropriate care](problems/health/PWS-0204.md), [pain](problems/health/PWS-0205.md) and [older people's independence](problems/care/PWS-0214.md) already ask what people experience. Research into repair and rejuvenation should retain those questions. Which findings demonstrate meaningful function or fewer illnesses, and which establish only a laboratory change? A useful first contribution is to reconcile trial registrations and publications so that null results, missing outcomes and adverse events remain visible alongside promising findings.

### Explore and inhabit the universe

Exploration extends the current project's scope. Its proposed human purposes are discovery, participation in science and more choices about where and how to live. Those purposes deserve discussion alongside costs and competing needs on Earth.

Warp travel, Orbitals and Dyson structures name different dreams: crossing stellar distances, creating places to live, and collecting stellar energy. Start by separating the problems. An auditable probe calculation can reveal travel times and energy requirements; a habitat study must address life support and human autonomy; a collector study must address materials, control and heat. Existing work on [learning](problems/learning/PWS-0216.md) can support participation in discovery, but it does not already establish a mandate for settlement. New physics must explain observations and survive tests; a desired destination cannot guarantee the laws allow a route.

### Create, play and celebrate

People should have room to make music, dance to celebrate the universe, tell stories, encounter beauty and explore spiritual meaning together. These experiences need no claim that they improve productivity to be worth pursuing.

The briefs on [relationships](problems/belonging/PWS-0219.md) and [time together](problems/care/PWS-0215.md) offer connections, while shared creativity is also valuable to people who are not lonely. A useful question is whether participant-directed tools remove cost, skill or access barriers without taking over creative choices. Compare an AI-supported activity with an equally well-supported conventional one. Ask participants what mattered; count exclusion and unwanted pressure, not just attendance.

## What fiction contributes, and what it cannot establish

In [Iain M. Banks's *A Few Notes on the Culture* (1994)](https://teagmhail.github.io/the-culture/essays/a-few-notes/), abundance accompanies freedom from compulsory labour, optional bodily changes, long lives and curiosity. His drug glands imagine voluntary control over experience; Orbitals imagine constructed living space. These are prompts for asking whose choices technology expands, not evidence for a biological or structural design.

Star Trek's holodeck offers a setting for shared stories, recreation and practice. The [official discussion of the concept](https://www.startrek.com/news/begin-program-the-reality-of-building-a-holodeck-today) describes its fictional combination of technologies. Immersive software can pursue some of those experiences without claiming to reproduce the fictional apparatus.

## Keep the evidence boundary visible

Attach the distinction to each claim, rather than assigning a single confidence label to an entire field:

| Kind of claim | What a contributor must show |
|---|---|
| Demonstrated capability | The experiment, conditions and measured result. [Positioning xenon atoms on nickel at 4 K](https://www.nature.com/articles/344524a0) is a specific demonstration, not general-purpose replication. |
| Research hypothesis | A proposed mechanism, a comparison that could disconfirm it, and the limits of the available evidence. |
| Speculative possibility | The missing physical or engineering assumptions. [Warp-drive energy-condition constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03079) must remain visible alongside the ambition. |

Supporting research should identify a decision, provide inspectable evidence or calculations, and end with a bounded next investigation. It creates no new territory or status and does not count as a validated problem. New briefs still follow the [contribution guide](CONTRIBUTING.md) and [measurement rules](METHODOLOGY.md).

The first review wanted is whether these ambitions expand the choices people value, and which connections to everyday life are missing or unconvincing. No affected-person review, scientific endorsement or project commitment is claimed here.
