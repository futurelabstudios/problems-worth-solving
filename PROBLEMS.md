# Problems AI Should Help Humanity Solve

This is version 0.1 of the map.

It is deliberately global. A person in Bengaluru, Lagos, London, São Paulo or a small town should be able to recognise many of these problems in their own life.

The targets below are provocations. They turn aspirations into falsifiable challenges. Contributors should improve them.

---

## 1. BODY — help every person remain healthy for longer

### 1.1 End the diagnostic maze

**Everyday reality:** People repeat their story to multiple doctors, undergo disconnected tests and sometimes wait months or years for the right diagnosis.

**AI-shaped bottleneck:** No human can continuously integrate a lifetime of symptoms, records, imaging, genetics, environment and evolving medical literature.

**Measurable challenges:**

- Reduce median time from first presentation to correct diagnosis by **80%**.
- Reduce serious diagnostic errors by **50%** without increasing unnecessary tests.
- Produce a usable differential diagnosis for common conditions within **10 minutes**.
- Make performance consistent across sex, age, skin tone, language and geography.
- Give patients and clinicians a calibrated explanation of uncertainty.

### 1.2 Detect disease before it becomes suffering

**Everyday reality:** Cancer, cardiovascular disease, metabolic illness and neurodegeneration are often discovered after damage has accumulated.

**Measurable challenges:**

- Detect high-burden disease materially earlier than current standard practice.
- Keep false-positive cascades below a defined harm and cost threshold.
- Personalise screening intervals using changing risk rather than population averages.
- Prove that earlier detection improves outcomes—not merely detection counts.

### 1.3 Prevent medication and treatment harm

- Reconcile a complete medication list across providers.
- Detect dangerous interactions, dosage errors and contraindications before treatment.
- Reduce severe preventable medication harm by **50%**.
- Keep low-value alert volume low enough that clinicians do not ignore the system.

### 1.4 Give every person a continuously improving health model

- Convert fragmented records and daily signals into a portable, person-controlled health history.
- Predict meaningful deterioration early enough to act.
- Recommend the smallest evidence-backed action likely to help.
- Distinguish “watch,” “book an appointment” and “seek urgent care” with clinically validated reliability.

### 1.5 Compress the path from molecule to medicine

- Cut time from target identification to viable clinical candidate by **10×**.
- Reduce the cost of failed candidates through earlier prediction.
- Improve trial recruitment and representation without exploiting patient data.
- Make rare and commercially neglected conditions economically searchable.

## 2. MIND — give everyone access to extraordinary cognition

### 2.1 A personal tutor for every learner

**Everyday reality:** Education moves at the speed of the class, not the learner.

**Measurable challenges:**

- Diagnose the exact prerequisite a learner is missing within **15 minutes**.
- Produce durable learning gains at less than **1%** of the cost of human one-to-one tutoring.
- Demonstrate retention and transfer six months later—not just quiz performance.
- Work in the learner’s language, examples and cultural context.
- Improve curiosity and confidence rather than creating dependence.

### 2.2 Make expertise universally affordable

Today, good legal, financial, technical, medical and business advice is rationed by geography and money.

- Complete bounded expert tasks at or above a competent professional baseline.
- Reduce the cost of first-pass expert help by **100×**.
- State uncertainty and route high-risk cases to accountable humans.
- Make expert knowledge usable through voice and local languages.

### 2.3 Build tools that improve thinking—not merely answer questions

- Help people identify hidden assumptions and missing evidence.
- Generate strong opposing arguments before important decisions.
- Measure whether users make better calibrated decisions over time.
- Prevent cognitive outsourcing by requiring active judgment where it matters.

### 2.4 Remove language as a limit on human possibility

- Translate speech, text and technical knowledge with near-human fidelity across the world’s languages.
- Preserve intent, humour, emotion and cultural meaning.
- Support languages with little digital data.
- Make real-time multilingual collaboration effectively frictionless.

## 3. TIME — return the finite hours of a human life

### 3.1 Eliminate routine life administration

**Everyday reality:** Bills, claims, taxes, forms, appointments, renewals, cancellations and document chasing consume attention without creating meaning.

**Measurable challenges:**

- Reduce time spent on routine personal administration by **90%**.
- Complete tasks end-to-end, not merely explain the steps.
- Maintain a complete audit trail and allow one-click reversal.
- Never make a payment, commitment or disclosure beyond explicit authority.
- Work across fragmented institutions without trapping users in one platform.

### 3.2 End low-value knowledge work

- Reduce time spent searching, formatting, copying, reconciling and status-reporting by **80%**.
- Preserve the decisions, relationships, taste and accountability that require humans.
- Measure useful output and error—not tokens generated or hours “saved.”
- Ensure productivity gains translate into higher human agency, not simply more surveillance and workload.

### 3.3 Make every meeting earn its existence

- Determine when a meeting is unnecessary.
- Give participants the context they need before it begins.
- Convert discussion into decisions, owners and follow-through.
- Reduce recurring meeting hours by **50%** without reducing coordination quality.

## 4. MATERIAL WORLD — make abundance cheaper and cleaner

### 4.1 Make clean energy reliably abundant

AI cannot repeal physics, but it can improve how we discover materials, forecast demand and operate complex grids.

- Improve renewable forecasting and reduce curtailment.
- Discover better batteries, catalysts and grid materials faster.
- Optimise demand without quietly reducing people’s comfort or autonomy.
- Reduce the delivered cost of reliable low-carbon electricity.
- Shorten grid interconnection and infrastructure-planning cycles.

### 4.2 Make housing radically easier to build

**Everyday reality:** Housing is unaffordable in many places partly because design, compliance, permitting, procurement and construction coordination remain slow and fragmented.

**Measurable challenges:**

- Reduce pre-construction time for code-compliant housing by **50%**.
- Cut design, estimation and permitting cost by **50%**.
- Detect safety and compliance errors before construction.
- Reduce material waste and schedule overruns.
- Generate locally appropriate designs rather than universal boxes.

### 4.3 Waste far less food

- Predict demand at farm, warehouse, store and household level.
- Match surplus to buyers or beneficiaries before spoilage.
- Reduce avoidable supply-chain food loss by **50%** in a defined system.
- Optimise shelf life, routing and pricing without shifting risk onto farmers.
- Help households waste less without requiring constant manual tracking.

### 4.4 Discover better materials for civilisation

Priority search spaces include energy storage, low-carbon cement and steel, cooling, water purification, fertiliser and recyclable polymers.

- Reduce the number of physical experiments needed to reach a target property.
- Make model-proposed materials synthesizable, safe and economical.
- Publish failed experiments so global research does not repeat them.
- Shorten discovery-to-deployment time by an order of magnitude.

### 4.5 Make clean water easier to find, produce and preserve

- Detect leakage and contamination before visible failure.
- Forecast local supply and demand under climate volatility.
- Optimise treatment and desalination energy.
- Give households trustworthy, real-time information about water safety.

## 5. ECONOMIC AGENCY — let more people create value

### 5.1 Give every small business a world-class operating team

**Everyday reality:** A small-business owner is expected to be strategist, marketer, accountant, recruiter, analyst and operations manager at once.

**Measurable challenges:**

- Produce reliable cash-flow forecasts and early warnings.
- Find the highest-value operational constraint each week.
- Automate bookkeeping, compliance and routine customer communication.
- Improve survival, margin or growth in controlled real-world comparisons.
- Cost less than **1% of revenue** for a typical small business.

### 5.2 Match people to opportunity through capability, not pedigree

- Infer demonstrated skills from work, not only degrees and job titles.
- Match people to roles, projects and collaborators they can actually succeed in.
- Recommend the shortest credible learning path to a better opportunity.
- Reduce time-to-hire while improving retention and performance.
- Test and prevent discrimination hidden in historical employment data.

### 5.3 Give every person a private economic navigator

- Identify avoidable fees, interest, subscriptions and tax leakage.
- Explain financial trade-offs in plain language.
- Improve household financial resilience, not trading frequency.
- Operate under a fiduciary-like duty to the user rather than commission incentives.
- Prevent action beyond a clearly delegated limit.

### 5.4 Make organisations feel ten times more capable without becoming inhuman

- Let small teams coordinate research, drafting, analysis, QA and follow-through.
- Preserve clear human ownership for judgment and consequences.
- Reduce coordination overhead as organisations grow.
- Measure whether quality and speed rise together.
- Ensure the gains do not depend on invisible worker exploitation.

## 6. TRUST AND CONNECTION — help people navigate other minds

### 6.1 Make digital fraud far harder

- Detect scams before money or credentials move.
- Explain the suspicious pattern in language the user understands.
- Keep false blocks low enough that ordinary commerce remains usable.
- Adapt to novel fraud without mass surveillance.
- Give vulnerable users optional, graduated safeguards.

### 6.2 Build a trustworthy layer over the information world

- Trace important claims to inspectable evidence.
- Distinguish fact, inference, prediction and persuasion.
- Show the strongest credible disagreement.
- Detect coordinated manipulation without declaring one central authority the owner of truth.
- Measure whether people become better calibrated—not merely more aligned with the system.

### 6.3 Reduce preventable loneliness without replacing human beings

- Detect when someone may need connection while respecting privacy.
- Help people maintain friendships and family relationships.
- Match people into durable communities around care, place and purpose.
- Route severe distress to capable humans.
- Measure real-world relationship strength, not time spent talking to a bot.

### 6.4 Make difficult conversations more possible

- Help people understand what another party is actually trying to protect.
- Translate across language, status and ideological frames.
- Reduce escalation in workplace, family and community disputes.
- Never impersonate consent or manipulate one party for another.

## 7. CIVILISATION — coordinate systems no individual can hold in mind

### 7.1 Make bureaucracy legible and executable

- Convert rules into personalised, verifiable next actions.
- Complete routine government and institutional processes in minutes rather than days.
- Detect contradictory rules and recurring failure points.
- Preserve explanation, appeal and accountable human authority.
- Reduce the cost of accessing a right, permit or service by **90%**.

### 7.2 Predict and coordinate disaster response

- Improve local forecasts for floods, fires, heat, storms and supply disruptions.
- Turn forecasts into decisions for specific people and infrastructure.
- Optimise evacuation, relief inventory and routing under uncertainty.
- Measure lead time, lives protected and false-alarm cost.
- Design for degraded connectivity and damaged infrastructure.

### 7.3 Help cities behave like coherent systems

- Coordinate transport, energy, water, waste, construction and emergency response.
- Reduce travel time, outages, leakage and pollution without expanding unaccountable surveillance.
- Test policy in simulations before imposing it on residents.
- Optimise for human experience, not a single dashboard metric.

### 7.4 Make public policy learn faster

- Find comparable interventions and their actual results.
- Model distributional and second-order effects.
- Identify what evidence would most change a decision.
- Run small, ethical experiments before national-scale rollout.
- Publish failure and uncertainty rather than manufacturing confidence.

### 7.5 Improve humanity’s ability to coordinate across borders

Apply intelligence to pandemic detection, climate adaptation, supply-chain fragility, cyber risk and other problems where every country acting alone produces a worse result.

- Detect emerging shared risks earlier.
- Make commitments and compliance easier to verify.
- Model incentives that make cooperation rational.
- Preserve plural values rather than forcing one global objective function.

## 8. FRONTIER — expand the space of possible futures

### 8.1 Build a memory for science

**Everyday reality:** Humanity repeatedly loses knowledge inside papers, failed experiments, incompatible datasets and retiring experts.

- Make scientific claims, methods, data and contradictions machine-navigable.
- Connect a question to all relevant positive and negative evidence.
- Detect irreproducible or internally inconsistent results.
- Preserve tacit experimental knowledge.
- Ensure access beyond wealthy institutions.

### 8.2 Turn scientific literature into testable hypotheses

- Generate hypotheses that experts judge non-obvious and plausible.
- State the mechanism and evidence that would falsify each one.
- Rank experiments by expected information gain, cost and safety.
- Measure success through validated discovery—not benchmark eloquence.

### 8.3 Automate laboratories safely

- Close the loop from hypothesis to experiment to observation to revision.
- Increase experiments per researcher without lowering quality.
- Record every parameter and failure for reproducibility.
- Apply strict containment and human oversight in dangerous domains.

### 8.4 Help humanity understand complex systems

Targets include cells, brains, ecosystems, economies and climate.

- Discover causal structure rather than surface correlation.
- Produce models that survive intervention and distribution shift.
- Identify what remains unknowable from current data.
- Make explanations usable by domain experts and decision-makers.

### 8.5 Expand humanity beyond Earth responsibly

- Improve autonomous science and operations where communication delays prevent constant human control.
- Design closed-loop life-support, manufacturing and repair systems.
- Reduce the cost and risk of scientific missions.
- Treat planetary protection and long-term governance as engineering requirements—not afterthoughts.

# Cross-cutting tests

A proposed problem or solution should answer:

1. **Would solving this materially improve an ordinary life?**
2. **Is intelligence genuinely a bottleneck?**
3. **Can the problem be decomposed into independently measurable parts?**
4. **Can success be measured in the real world, not only on a benchmark?**
5. **Who gains, who pays and who might be harmed?**
6. **Does the system increase human agency or quietly remove it?**
7. **Could a solution scale across languages, cultures and income levels?**
8. **What happens when it is wrong?**
9. **Is there a credible path from prototype to deployment?**
10. **Are we solving the problem—or optimising a proxy?**

# What is missing?

Almost certainly a lot.

Open an issue or pull request if a universal problem is absent, a target is naïve, a category hides important trade-offs or an apparently promising challenge should be rejected entirely.
