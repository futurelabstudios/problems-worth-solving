# Problems Worth Solving

> We may soon have a country of geniuses in a datacenter.  
> Who decides what they work on?

For decades we have made lists of the most influential people on Earth.

This is a list of the problems worthy of their intelligence.

## The premise

For most of history, intelligence was scarce. Experts were rare, worked limited hours and communicated through narrow channels.

That constraint is disappearing.

AI can increasingly reason, research, predict, design, code, simulate and coordinate. Millions of agents may soon work continuously and in parallel. But abundant intelligence does not automatically create an abundant world.

Left entirely to the market, enormous intelligence will optimise advertising, speculation and engagement because those problems already have clear owners and business models.

The harder question is:

**What should powerful intelligence solve for humanity—and how would we know it had succeeded?**

## What this repository is

**Problems Worth Solving** is an open map of critical human problems that AI is unusually capable of helping solve.

The list begins from first principles:

- Humans need health, agency, knowledge, time, shelter, energy, food, safety and connection.
- Civilisation needs truth, coordination, resilience, discovery and a habitable planet.
- AI is most useful where the bottleneck is cognition: prediction, personalisation, search, design, simulation, translation or coordination.
- A large aspiration becomes useful only when it is broken into measurable problems.

This is not primarily a nonprofit list.

Some problems will be solved by startups. Some by governments. Some by scientists, open-source communities, large companies or strange new coalitions. Many will create enormous economic value precisely because they create enormous human value.

## The first-principles problem map

Read the initial list:

It covers eight universal territories:

1. **Body** — staying healthy and living longer
2. **Mind** — learning, thinking and creating
3. **Time** — escaping administrative and cognitive drudgery
4. **Material world** — affordable energy, food and shelter
5. **Economic agency** — earning, building and running organisations
6. **Trust and connection** — truth, safety and human relationships
7. **Civilisation** — coordination, resilience and better institutions
8. **Frontier** — accelerating science and expanding what is possible

## What makes a problem belong here?

A problem should be:

- **Universal or widely transferable** — recognisable across countries and cultures
- **Consequential** — solving it materially changes lives or civilisation
- **AI-shaped** — intelligence is a meaningful bottleneck
- **Decomposable** — it can be broken into solvable parts
- **Measurable** — success and failure can be observed
- **Open to competing approaches** — the problem is not a disguised product pitch
- **Safe enough to attempt** — risks, rights and accountability can be designed in

## From vague ambition to measurable challenge

“Fix healthcare” is not a usable problem.

These are:

- Cut the median time from first symptom to correct diagnosis by 80%.
- Give any learner a tutor that produces durable mastery at less than 1% of the cost of human one-to-one tutoring.
- Reduce the time required to complete routine life administration by 90%.
- Reduce the cost and development time of a validated drug candidate by 10×.
- Detect digital fraud before money moves while holding false blocks below an agreed threshold.
- Cut the cost and time of designing and permitting a safe home by 50%.

The targets are hypotheses, not commandments. They exist so contributors can improve them.

## A problem—not a product

“Build an AI doctor” is a product idea.

“People wait months or years for the correct diagnosis because no one can integrate their complete history, symptoms, tests and medical knowledge” is a problem.

Starting with the problem keeps the solution space open. The answer may be an AI system, a better workflow, a new institution, physical infrastructure—or a combination.

## How to contribute

- Propose a missing problem.
- Break an existing problem into more precise sub-problems.
- Replace an attractive but meaningless metric.
- Add evidence, datasets or prior attempts.
- Explain a second-order consequence.
- Build and benchmark a solution.
- Commission a challenge.
- Argue, with evidence, that something does **not** belong on the list.

Read [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) and use the [problem template](templates/problem-template.md).

## The standard

Each mature problem entry should identify:

- the ordinary human experience;
- the first-principles constraint;
- why AI changes what is possible;
- measurable sub-problems;
- baseline and target;
- available and missing data;
- failure modes;
- who can build, buy, fund or regulate solutions;
- and the smallest serious experiment.

## Principles

1. **Human value before technological spectacle.**
2. **Problems before products.**
3. **Measurable progress before impressive demos.**
4. **Abundance before artificial scarcity.**
5. **Agency before automation.**
6. **Evidence before virality.**
7. **Open disagreement before fake consensus.**
8. **The goal is not to make AI look intelligent. The goal is to make life meaningfully better.**

## Origin

The project began with a reaction to the idea of “a country of geniuses in a datacenter”:

> Who is commissioning the ever-growing list of problems these geniuses are demonstrably solving? Wouldn’t it be the most useful list on a planet busy making “influential people” lists for decades?

This repository is the beginning of that list.
