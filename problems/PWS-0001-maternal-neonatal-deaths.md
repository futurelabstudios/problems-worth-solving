# PWS-0001: Prevent avoidable maternal and neonatal deaths

## In one sentence

Pregnant women and newborns still die from complications that could often be detected and treated if risk, referral and readiness aligned in time.

## The human reality

A woman can attend antenatal visits and still reach a facility whose staff lack her history, blood, transport or an available specialist. The fatal failure is rarely one missing fact. It is a chain of small delays: recognising danger, deciding to seek care, reaching care and receiving appropriate care.

## Why it matters

WHO states that most maternal deaths are preventable and that the burden remains concentrated in low-income and fragile settings. Every maternal death also reshapes the health, income and future of a family.

## System boundary

**Included:** Antenatal risk detection, referral, facility readiness, emergency response and the first 28 days of newborn life.  
**Excluded:** General fertility services and long-term child development.  
**Unit:** District health system and mother–newborn pair.

## Evidence

- WHO: [Maternal mortality](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/maternal-mortality)
- WHO: [Newborn mortality](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/newborn-mortality)
- India data sources include HMIS, Sample Registration System reports and state maternal-death reviews.
- Critical uncertainty: routine data quality and under-reporting vary sharply by place.

## Desired outcome

Reduce preventable maternal and neonatal deaths in a defined district without increasing unnecessary intervention, exclusion or surveillance.

## What blocks progress

Fragmented records; weak prediction from sparse data; delayed escalation; ambulance and blood availability; uneven clinical capacity; fear of blame; and dashboards that describe the past without changing the next decision.

## Role for intelligence

- Identify risk patterns across longitudinal records.
- Translate protocols into point-of-care decision support.
- Anticipate facility, blood and referral bottlenecks.
- Help review near-misses and deaths for system learning.
- Preserve final clinical authority with accountable professionals.

## Commissioning map

- **Authority:** State health departments and district health administrations
- **Funders:** Governments, multilaterals, CSR and health foundations
- **Hosts:** District hospitals, primary-health networks and ambulance systems
- **Essential voices:** Mothers, ASHAs, nurses, obstetricians and families

## Risks and safeguards

False reassurance, alarm fatigue, biased risk scores, coercive care and privacy failures. Require local validation, human override, auditable recommendations and prohibition on using risk scores to deny care.

## Evaluation

Primary outcomes: severe maternal outcomes and neonatal mortality. Leading indicators: referral completion, time-to-care and emergency readiness. Counter-metrics: unnecessary referral, caesarean rate, staff burden and inequitable error rates.

## Smallest meaningful next step

Select one district; reconstruct 100 recent severe outcomes and near-misses; identify the three most common preventable decision failures; design one prospective intervention with mothers and frontline staff.
