# PWS-0003: Find people excluded from benefits they are eligible for

## In one sentence

People routinely miss public benefits because eligibility rules, records, documentation and application processes do not meet around the citizen.

## The human reality

The poorest person often faces the highest administrative burden: discovering a scheme, interpreting eligibility, collecting documents, travelling to an office, correcting mismatched records and repeatedly proving need.

## Desired outcome

Increase correct enrolment and successful benefit receipt among eligible people, without expanding surveillance or wrongly excluding existing recipients.

## System boundary

Focus on one geography and a small family of schemes—such as pensions, maternal benefits or scholarships—rather than every benefit at once.

## Evidence

India’s [Direct Benefit Transfer portal](https://dbtbharat.gov.in/) and scheme portals show the breadth of the system. The key evidence gap is not the number of schemes but reliable measurement of eligible non-recipients and the reasons for exclusion.

## What has been tried

Single-window portals, camps, community workers, automatic enrolment and digitised identity or payment systems. Digital access helps many people while creating new failure modes around records, authentication and grievance resolution.

## What blocks progress

Rules written for programmes rather than people; siloed datasets; inconsistent names and addresses; low awareness; documentation gaps; language and disability barriers; and no owner for the citizen’s end-to-end outcome.

## Role for intelligence

Translate rules into plain language, identify likely eligibility with consent, detect missing documents, route grievances and reveal recurring exclusion patterns. AI should not make opaque final eligibility decisions.

## Commissioning map

- **Authority:** Relevant ministries, state departments and district administrations
- **Hosts:** Common Service Centres, panchayats and trusted civil-society organisations
- **Funders:** Government, philanthropy and development agencies
- **Essential voices:** Claimants, frontline workers, disability groups and grievance officers

## Risks and safeguards

Mass profiling, data linkage beyond consent, false eligibility decisions and automated denial. Begin with citizen-controlled assistance and aggregate gap analysis; preserve appeal, explanation and accountable human review.

## Evaluation

Successful benefit receipt among eligible non-recipients; time and cost to apply; grievance resolution; incorrect recommendation rate; and outcomes across gender, caste, disability and remoteness.

## Smallest meaningful next step

Choose one district and three schemes. With a trusted local organisation, interview 100 likely eligible non-recipients, map the exclusion journey and test a consent-based eligibility and grievance assistant.
