# PWS-0004: Diagnose and close foundational learning gaps

## In one sentence

Children in the same classroom learn at widely different levels, while one teacher lacks the time and tools to diagnose and respond to each child.

## The human reality

A child who misses an early concept is promoted into material that assumes mastery. The gap compounds. Eventually the child may be labelled weak, even though the system has rarely provided instruction at the level they actually occupy.

## Evidence

[ASER](https://asercentre.org/) repeatedly documents foundational reading and arithmetic outcomes among rural Indian children. India’s [NIPUN Bharat](https://nipunbharat.education.gov.in/) establishes foundational literacy and numeracy as a national priority.

## Desired outcome

More children acquire durable, grade-appropriate foundational literacy and numeracy—without narrowing education to test performance or shifting responsibility from systems onto children.

## What has been tried

Teaching at the Right Level, structured pedagogy, tutoring, teacher coaching, adaptive software and periodic assessment. Many work under some conditions; scaling with fidelity and fitting real classroom workflows remain difficult.

## What blocks progress

Large mixed-level classes; infrequent assessment; teacher workload; limited remediation time; language mismatch; poor connectivity; generic content; and weak feedback from assessment into tomorrow’s lesson.

## Role for intelligence

Generate short adaptive assessments, group children by current need, propose classroom activities, translate content and help teachers see change over time. The teacher remains the relational and accountable centre.

## Commissioning map

Education departments, school networks, foundations, CSR programmes and learning organisations, with teachers, parents and children represented in design.

## Risks and safeguards

Excess testing, stigma, surveillance, cultural mismatch and over-reliance on screens. Collect minimal data, make grouping temporary, test in local languages and measure curiosity and attendance alongside competency.

## Evaluation

Independent reading and numeracy gains; retention after six months; teacher preparation time; attendance; student confidence; and gaps by gender, language, disability and socioeconomic status.

## Smallest meaningful next step

Partner with ten schools. Give teachers a five-minute diagnostic and next-day grouping plan in the local language; compare learning gain and teacher workload against ordinary practice for one term.
