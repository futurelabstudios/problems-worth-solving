# PWS-0005: Warn small farmers about crop outbreaks early enough to act

## In one sentence

Small farmers often learn that a pest or disease is spreading only after crop damage is visible and the cheapest response window has closed.

## The human reality

The cost is not merely lost yield. A failed crop can mean debt, distress sale of assets and reduced spending on food, health and education. Advice that arrives late—or recommends the wrong chemical—can deepen the harm.

## Evidence

FAO describes transboundary plant pests and diseases as threats to food security and livelihoods: [FAO plant production and protection](https://www.fao.org/plant-production-protection/en/). Local prevalence and loss must be established crop by crop.

## Desired outcome

Increase the share of farmers receiving accurate, local and actionable warnings before economically significant damage, while reducing unnecessary pesticide use.

## What blocks progress

Sparse field surveillance; delayed reporting; local microclimates; poor diagnostic images; fragmented weather, crop and market data; last-mile language; and advice influenced by input sales.

## Role for intelligence

Combine field reports, weather, satellite signals and farmer images; detect anomalous clusters; estimate local spread; and translate integrated-pest-management guidance into local language.

## Data and access

Crop calendars, geolocated field observations, weather, remote sensing, laboratory confirmation and intervention outcomes. Protect farmer location and commercial data; separate advice from input-selling incentives.

## Commissioning map

Agriculture departments, insurers, cooperatives, food companies, research institutes and farmer-producer organisations.

## Risks and safeguards

False alarms can cause costly spraying; missed alarms destroy trust; recommendations may harm ecosystems. Use confidence bands, agronomist validation, non-chemical options and independent evaluation of pesticide use.

## Evaluation

Warning lead time; confirmed precision/recall; avoided yield loss; farmer action; pesticide expenditure and toxicity; false-alert cost; and performance for marginal farmers.

## Smallest meaningful next step

Choose one crop and district with a recurrent outbreak. Combine extension-worker observations, weather and farmer reports for one season; measure whether warnings arrive earlier than the existing channel and change a verified decision.
