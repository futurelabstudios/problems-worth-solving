# Keep the lights on and the home comfortable without bill shock

> I should not have to choose between a comfortable home and paying the bill.

[All problems](../../CATALOGUE.md) · Afford the basics · PWS-0203

## What a better life would feel like

Reliable electricity, tolerable indoor temperatures and predictable bills, including for people who rent.

## The problem, in measurable parts

| What needs to change | What we would measure |
|---|---|
| Lower the real bill | Household energy cost after equipment, subscription and financing costs. |
| Preserve comfort | Hours outside resident-agreed temperature ranges and self-reported discomfort. |
| Reduce worry | Unexpected bill increases, disconnections and time spent managing energy. |

Start by asking people living with this problem whether these outcomes matter to them. Measure both their experience and observable conditions; neither alone tells the whole story. Report differences across households and circumstances, not just an average.

## Where AI could help

Forecast bills, explain tariff choices and coordinate appliances or heating within resident-set limits.

## What technology cannot fix on its own

Poor insulation, energy prices, unreliable supply and landlord decisions can overwhelm control improvements.

## How the benefit must reach the person

Use actual household bills and resident feedback. Reject savings captured entirely by the energy provider or offset by device costs.

**Benefit chain to verify:** technical improvement → a changed service, price or practical option → a measurable improvement in someone's life. A break anywhere in that chain means the human outcome has not been demonstrated.

## A first experiment

Compare an eight-week resident-approved scheduling service with tariff guidance and well-set timers. Proposed gate: 10% lower weather-adjusted energy cost with no worse comfort and no more than five minutes of weekly management. Treat seasonal results as local evidence.

This experiment tests one part of the larger ambition. Passing it does not mean the entire problem has been solved.

**Evidence status:** Draft hypothesis. No local baseline, data access, commissioner, pilot or achieved effect is claimed. Numerical thresholds are proposed design choices, not evidence-based predictions. Establish baseline and feasibility with participants before setting final targets.

## What is needed to test it

A host with access to the relevant service or setting; people who experience the problem; a domain specialist; consented outcome and cost records; and an independent evaluator. These are requirements, not resources already secured.

Before enrolment, name the sample, comparator, measurement period, full cost and expected uncertainty. Use only necessary data; confirm permissions and provide a practical route to withdraw. For clinical or other high-stakes experiments, obtain appropriate professional and institutional review.

## Who might make it happen

The organisations and services involved in the first experiment are possible hosts or buyers, not committed partners. Public funding, a sustainable business or a cooperative model may all be appropriate. The payment model must leave a demonstrable benefit with the person after fees, travel, equipment, attention and unpaid work.

## What could make life worse

No hidden temperature restrictions, unsafe appliance control or automatic tariff commitments; retain simple manual override.

## Stop or rethink if

The practical benefit fails to reach people; review and setup consume the claimed savings; the approach shifts cost or work onto someone with less power; or important harms increase. Also test whether the stated comparator achieves the same human outcome more simply.

A short-term process gain cannot stand in for a long-term health, income, relationship or safety outcome. Publish failed and abandoned cases as well as successes.

## Making this relevant in different lives

Include cooling and heating needs, prepaid meters, outages, shared accommodation and low-connectivity options.

There is no single average household. Validate with people from different income levels, family structures and access conditions before claiming broad usefulness.

## First contribution wanted

Tell us whether this describes your experience, what a meaningful improvement would be, and what the proposed measures miss. Then help establish a baseline. No impact score is assigned before that work.
