# Keep the health and independence to continue the life you choose

> Illustrative wish, not a research quote: “I want more years when I can keep doing what matters to me.”

[All problems](../../CATALOGUE.md) · Feel well · PWS-0227

## What a better life would feel like

A person retains the function to pursue relationships, work, play and chosen activities as they grow older. Disease, treatment burden and loss of independence consume less of their life. Effective help, when demonstrated, is accessible without exhausting the household's resources.

The long-term ambition includes defeating disease, ageing and involuntary death. This is a direction for inquiry, not a prediction or a claim that anyone can achieve immortality. People retain choices about care and life extension. Disability does not make a life less valuable, and independence can include support chosen by the person.

This adds the longer-term loss and preservation of function to the existing briefs on [getting appropriate care](PWS-0204.md), [pain](PWS-0205.md) and [supporting older parents](../care/PWS-0214.md). It does not replace care that people need now.

## The problem, in measurable parts

| What needs to change | What we would measure |
|---|---|
| Retain meaningful function | Person-chosen activities paired with appropriate validated physical or cognitive measures; changes in assistance wanted or needed. |
| Reduce disease and treatment burden | Relevant clinical events, symptoms, hospital time and treatment adverse effects, with stated follow-up and denominators. |
| Make effective help accessible | Eligibility, waiting time, total out-of-pocket and caregiver costs, and participant-reported ability to obtain and choose care. |

A biomarker change belongs in the research record. It cannot substitute for these outcomes without evidence that it predicts the benefit claimed. An apparent improvement in one measure cannot erase worsened symptoms, adverse events or exclusion.

## Where AI could help

Research assistance may help locate, reconcile and extract evidence across trial registrations, protocols and publications. Its useful output is a more accurate and complete account of what was tested and found. Structured manual extraction is the comparator. A qualified person checks clinically material claims; generating a plausible summary is insufficient.

## What technology cannot fix on its own

Biological uncertainty, lengthy follow-up, access to care and incentives to report only favorable results remain. Faster research does not guarantee a successful treatment or fair access. New evidence must change the care people can actually receive before research efficiency counts as a demonstrated health benefit.

Banks's [fictional drug glands](https://teagmhail.github.io/the-culture/essays/a-few-notes/) imagine voluntary control over experience. They motivate questions about bodily autonomy, but do not provide evidence for senolytics, safe mood control or biological rejuvenation. Those are distinct scientific questions.

## How the benefit must reach the person

More reliable evidence → better-founded research and care decisions → effective, acceptable and affordable interventions → meaningful function or less burden. This chain remains a hypothesis. The extraction exercise below tests only its first step. It offers no treatment advice and authorizes no intervention in people.

## A first experiment

### Reconcile human senolytic studies without losing null results

**Proposed document-study protocol; not executed.** Fix and publish a search date. Search ClinicalTrials.gov for `senolytic`, `dasatinib AND quercetin`, and `fisetin`, restricted to interventional studies, without restricting recruitment status. Preserve queries, timestamped exports and record histories. The registry is the bounded source, not an exhaustive inventory of global research.

Two reviewers independently screen records for human interventions explicitly intended to remove senescent cells. Include studies with no publication or posted results; exclude observational studies and interventions with another stated purpose. Resolve disagreement and publish reasons before choosing the evaluation corpus. Deduplicate by registry ID. Use the first 20 eligible IDs in ascending numeric order, or all if fewer; publish excluded eligible IDs. This reproducible sampling is a feasibility choice and may overrepresent earlier trials.

For each ID, search PubMed by exact registry ID and inspect registry-linked publications and protocols. Record queries and inaccessible sources. A publication without an identifier needs agreement on investigators, intervention, population and timing; uncertain matches stay unresolved. Save a reference manifest with source versions and retrieval dates, respecting licenses. Do not infer unpublished results from recruitment status.

Extract the following for every study, preserving disagreements between sources:

| Required fields | How to handle ambiguity |
|---|---|
| Population, enrollment and analyzed counts | Separate planned from actual numbers, by arm where available. |
| Design, comparator, intervention and follow-up | Record open-label/blinded status and duration for each reported endpoint. |
| Prespecified primary and secondary endpoints | Compare initial and amended registry/protocol records with publications; record changes and timing. |
| Results and uncertainty | Preserve units, denominators, estimates and reported intervals; distinguish prespecified from exploratory analyses. |
| Adverse events, withdrawals and missing results | “Not reported” is distinct from “none”; record absent outcomes and unavailable sources explicitly. |

Use two similarly qualified extraction teams. Randomly assign half the studies to AI-assisted extraction in team A and manual extraction in team B; reverse for the remainder. A person sees each study in only one condition. Both conditions receive identical source bundles and the same form. Record model/version, prompts and output corrections. Keep the adjudicators blind to extraction condition; use a clinical reviewer and an evidence-methods reviewer to resolve errors against sources.

Report clinically material errors per required field, completeness across all study-field pairs, and total human time per assigned study, including failed attempts and correction. An abstention counts as missing, not as accurate; retain any explicit evidence-supported “not reported” value. Predefine material errors as changes to population, comparison, endpoint status, direction/magnitude, safety or follow-up that could change interpretation.

The feasibility gate is lower total human time without increased material errors or omitted evidence relative to manual extraction. Report paired differences and uncertainty; 20 studies cannot establish broad safety or reliability. Any omitted serious adverse event or invented benefit triggers review before summaries are reused. Establish the corpus and adjudication rubric before setting a numeric improvement target.

## Evidence status: a deliberately small senescence appendix

These examples illustrate evidence levels, not a complete review or the current balance of all senolytic research. The proposed registry exercise is needed to find missing and subsequent results.

| Evidence level | Primary example and finding | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism and animal outcomes | [Baker et al. (2016)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16932) used an engineered cell-clearance system in mice and reported longer median lifespan and improvements in selected age-related outcomes. | A corresponding human treatment, uniform benefit across tissues, or elimination of ageing. |
| Human feasibility | [Justice et al. (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30616998/) report a 14-person open-label idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis pilot with feasibility endpoints, secondary functional improvements and one serious adverse event. | Without a control group, observed changes do not establish treatment efficacy or broad safety; the event alone does not establish causation. |
| Randomized human evidence with a null primary result | [Farr et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03096-2), 60 postmenopausal women, found no between-group difference in the primary 20-week bone-resorption marker endpoint. Exploratory subgroup findings were reported. The paper identifies [NCT04313634](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04313634). | Exploratory or biomarker findings do not establish fewer fractures, rejuvenation, improved survival or an effective anti-ageing treatment. |

No local baseline, affected-person review, completed extraction benchmark or achieved human benefit is claimed. The cited examples do not demonstrate defeat of death.

## What is needed to test it

A defined corpus, qualified extraction and adjudication teams, access to the required documents, a versioned extraction form and a budget for review time. None is secured. This uses public research documents; any later clinical study requires its own scientific and institutional review.

## Who might make it happen

An evidence-synthesis group, library or public-interest research funder could support the exercise. These are possible roles, not partners. Disclose interests in treatments and AI tools; funders must permit publication of null and negative findings.

## What could make life worse

An inaccurate summary can create false hope, amplify exploratory results or hide harms. Commercial claims may imply that a measurable laboratory change delivers extra healthy years. Keep source passages and uncertainty inspectable; do not produce personalized treatment recommendations.

## Stop or rethink if

Source access prevents adjudication, material omissions persist, or manual extraction is equally useful with less burden. Restrict claims to the evaluated corpus; do not turn successful extraction into a health claim.

## Making this relevant in different lives

Patients and older people should choose which functions matter, including those living with disability or multiple conditions. Report trial exclusions and access barriers rather than generalizing from a selected study population.

## First contribution wanted

An ageing researcher and an evidence-methods reviewer should challenge the evidence distinctions and extraction rubric. People experiencing declining function should review whether the three human outcomes capture what they value.
