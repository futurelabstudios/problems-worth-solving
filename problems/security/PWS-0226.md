# Have the means to choose how you spend your life

> Illustrative wish, not a research quote: “If our tools can do more, I want more choice about how I live.”

[All problems](../../CATALOGUE.md) · Feel financially secure · PWS-0226

## What a better life would feel like

A person can refuse harmful work, care for someone, learn, create or change direction without immediately losing essentials. Access to productive capital and the income it produces can matter alongside earnings. People outside paid employment belong in this ambition too.

[PWS-0207](PWS-0207.md) addresses finding dependable, fairly paid work. This brief asks how gains from automation change a person's material options, including the option to work less. Economic measures do not establish spiritual fulfillment: freedom of conscience and the choice of spiritual practice, or none, remain with the person.

## The problem, in measurable parts

| What needs to change | What we would measure |
|---|---|
| Secure essentials while retaining options | Disposable household resources after taxes, benefit changes and essential costs; volatility, missed payments and liquidity. Separate income from inaccessible equity. |
| Gain time people can choose | Paid and unpaid work, commuting and recovery time; discretionary hours chosen by the person. Record work transferred to colleagues or family. |
| Increase practical agency | Participant-reported ability to decline unwanted work, change direction or pursue a chosen activity, paired with concrete examples they voluntarily provide. |

Measure employees, leavers and displaced workers, not just people retained after automation. Report who could not participate. A rise in average income can conceal losses for those with least bargaining power.

## Where AI could help

AI may reduce particular production tasks or help reconcile cost and time records. Test that contribution against a well-run existing process before attributing a surplus to it. Include supervision, errors and implementation effort. Deterministic accounting is the comparator for allocating a known gain; a model need not decide who deserves it.

## What technology cannot fix on its own

Control over assets, contracts, benefits, bargaining power and access to public services affect which choices are real. A company cannot establish society-wide freedom through an internal pilot. Consider four arrangements without presuming one is best everywhere:

| Arrangement | Who qualifies and decides | Who bears losses; who is outside |
|---|---|---|
| Higher pay | Covered workers; agreement between workers and employer | Employer bears a committed wage increase; insecure workers may still lose jobs. Nonworkers get no direct share. |
| Shorter hours at preserved pay | Covered workers choose workable schedules with the employer | Employer must sustain output or fund coverage; colleagues must not absorb hidden work. Nonworkers get no direct share. |
| Worker ownership or profit sharing | Eligible workers under explicit vesting, voting and distribution rules | Equity may lose value and profit shares may vanish. Ownership need not provide cash or control; former workers and contractors may be excluded. |
| Broadly shared capital income | A fund's defined beneficiary population; accountable allocation rules | Beneficiaries face variable distributions and fund losses. Nonworkers can qualify, but residency or other eligibility rules can still exclude people. |

## How the benefit must reach the person

### One gain, four illustrative allocations

**Planning assumptions, not observed AI effects:** a firm with 20 equally paid workers expects an annual €120,000 gross contribution gain after ordinary nonlabor production costs, before extra automation costs. Annualized setup costs €20,000 and ongoing software, maintenance and review cost €40,000. Net available gain: **€60,000/year**. Assume output and employment remain stable. Actual labor, tax and distribution arrangements require local costing.

Each row below allocates the same €60,000; they are alternatives, not cumulative benefits. All cash figures precede personal taxes and benefit interactions.

| Alternative | Explicit allocation | What a person receives |
|---|---|---|
| Pay | €60,000 / 20; assume no additional employer payroll costs for this illustration | €3,000/year gross each; recalculate take-home resources locally. |
| Time | Buy necessary replacement capacity at an assumed all-in €30/hour: €60,000 / €30 = 2,000 hours | 100 paid hours released per worker/year, about 2.17 hours/week across 46 working weeks. No extra cash is also distributed. |
| Profit sharing | An equal, immediately payable profit share allocates all €60,000 | €3,000/year each in this example; this grants neither equity nor voting rights. Equity-only awards must instead report liquidity and control. |
| Shared capital income | A hypothetical fund already entitled to this entire gain distributes €60,000 equally to 200 beneficiaries | €300/year each, including eligible nonworkers. Acquisition, administration and taxes are assumed zero here, not in a real fund. |

The time arrangement spends the gain on coverage; it does not also claim that the same released hours generated a second cash saving. If actual available gain is €30,000, all four allocations halve under these assumptions. An entitlement or funding mechanism must exist before treating any allocation as deliverable.

**Failure case:** two workers lose €30,000/year each and owners retain the gain. Increased company surplus does not establish freedom for those workers. Track their household resources and choices after departure; involuntary unemployment is not discretionary time. Likewise, releasing paid hours while requiring unpaid completion fails the time measure.

## A first experiment

Propose one consenting organization introducing automation to a bounded workflow. Establish eight weeks of baseline records; evaluate six months of implementation and follow up three months later. These are feasibility periods, not established durations for detecting durable effects.

Before launch, workers and management select one allocation arrangement and sign a plain-language account of eligible people, costs, payment or time-release rules, downside protection and dispute resolution. Preserve a versioned record. A reviewer independent of management reconciles reported gains with actual distributions.

Compare with matched teams continuing the existing workflow, matching task mix, pay, hours and demand where feasible. Record contemporaneous changes in demand, staffing and management. If teams volunteer or management selects them, differences cannot establish a causal AI effect; report the selection and baseline differences. Randomize phased introduction only if workable and agreed before outcomes are observed.

Collect the three human outcomes at baseline, monthly and follow-up. Include all initially eligible workers and consenting leavers; disclose missing follow-up and do not assign zero income or zero burden. Keep identifiable financial records private. Separately record contractor workload, customer service quality and unpaid family effort. Preregister a worker-chosen meaningful improvement and acceptable downside before launch; no unsupported percentage target is supplied here.

The feasibility decision is whether a verified gain reaches people on the agreed terms without worsened material security or hidden work. A simpler process with the same benefit is a valid result. This exercise does not compare all four institutions experimentally or validate a universal income policy.

## Evidence status

- [Fan et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02259-6) report wellbeing improvements in a six-month preserved-pay workweek intervention, using pre/post observations from 2,896 employees in 141 organizations and 12 control companies. The published abstract supports investigating hours and wellbeing; it does not establish random allocation, AI-generated gains or applicability to every workplace.
- [Buchele et al., *Show Me the Money* (2009)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14830) analyze employee surveys and report no evidence of stock ownership substituting for pay or benefits in those data. Survey associations do not guarantee that a proposed ownership scheme distributes gains fairly.
- [Kangas et al., Finnish basic-income evaluation (2020)](https://tietotarjotin.fi/en/publication/116455/evaluation-of-the-finnish-basic-income-experiment) studied a randomized unemployment-benefit population and reports better wellbeing on several survey measures. It does not test a universal capital dividend; survey responses and population limits matter.

These are selected primary evidence summaries, not a systematic review or AI-effect estimate. Local baselines, feedback and resources remain unestablished.

## What is needed to test it

A host, worker representatives, an independent evaluator, lawful access to cost/pay/time records and a funded allocation agreement. None is secured. Budget evaluation and worker participation time before calling the gain distributable.

## Who might make it happen

A firm or cooperative with worker participation could host; a research or public-interest commissioner could fund evaluation. Financial interests in the automation vendor and ownership arrangement must be disclosed.

## What could make life worse

Surveillance, retaliation for honest feedback, concentrated investment losses or benefit reductions can negate a nominal gain. Do not condition employment or payments on favorable survey responses.

## Stop or rethink if

Agreed distributions fail, unpaid work rises, security falls, or reported gains cannot be reconciled. Suspend collection if confidentiality cannot be protected. Publish negative results and differences between groups.

## Making this relevant in different lives

Include part-time and contract work in eligibility discussions. Separate analysis for caregivers, low-paid workers and leavers. A workplace pilot leaves nonworkers untested; report that boundary explicitly.

## First contribution wanted

Workers and a finance reviewer should challenge the allocation example and identify one real workflow where both costs and recipients can be followed.
