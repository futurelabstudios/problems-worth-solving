# Finish essential paperwork without losing a day

> I want to sort out a form, claim or renewal without taking time off work.

[All problems](../../CATALOGUE.md) · Have time to live · PWS-0212

## What a better life would feel like

People receive the service or correct resolution they need with less time, cost and confusion.

## The problem, in measurable parts

| What needs to change | What we would measure |
|---|---|
| Reduce personal effort | Active minutes, trips and wages lost per enrolled case, including corrections, abandoned and rejected cases. |
| Reach the outcome | Completion, correct resolution and actual service receipt reported separately; time to each, with unresolved cases counted at follow-up. |
| Preserve control | Applicant-reported understanding and control, alongside errors, unwanted commitments and ability to appeal a decision. |

Start by asking people living with this problem whether these outcomes matter to them. Measure both their experience and observable conditions; neither alone tells the whole story. Report differences across households and circumstances, not just an average.

## Where AI could help

Explain requirements, assemble supplied evidence, prefill forms and follow status with explicit authority.

## What technology cannot fix on its own

Bad rules, inaccessible institutions and under-resourced staff cannot always be solved by better form-filling.

## How the benefit must reach the person

Measure time and money saved by applicants, including correcting the AI. Processing savings inside an institution are secondary.

**Benefit chain to verify:** technical improvement → a changed service, price or practical option → a measurable improvement in someone's life. A break anywhere in that chain means the human outcome has not been demonstrated.

## A first experiment

Choose one common renewal or billing dispute in one local service. Run a 60–90-day feasibility comparison of AI-assisted completion with a clear checklist, with equivalent access to ordinary human help in both groups. Agree the allocation method and follow-up period before enrolment.

Count every case enrolled in the comparison, whether or not the person uses the assistance or finishes:

- Record applicant time spent gathering documents, checking answers, travelling, following up and correcting mistakes. Record wages lost and other costs separately.
- Record caregiver and service-worker time separately, so reduced applicant effort cannot conceal work transferred to someone else.
- Report completion, independently checked correctness of the resolution and actual service receipt against the full enrolled denominator. A submitted form alone is not success; an appropriate denial is distinct from failure to complete required steps.
- Report abandoned, rejected and still-pending cases, plus missing follow-up, separately. Do not drop them from the comparison or count missing effort records as zero. Ask applicants whether they understood and controlled the process.

**Provisional proposed gate:** 50% lower mean applicant active minutes per enrolled case, with no drop in completion or correct resolution and no deterioration in service receipt. Review this target with applicants and the host after establishing the baseline and before the comparison starts; it is not a research-derived effect prediction.

Report uncertainty for both effort and outcome comparisons. Faster abandonment is not a gain. An inconclusive outcome comparison does not establish that correctness or access was preserved and cannot pass the gate. Cases still awaiting a decision or service may need longer follow-up before claiming benefit.

This experiment tests one part of the larger ambition. Passing it does not mean the entire problem has been solved.

**Evidence status:** Draft hypothesis. No local baseline, data access, commissioner, pilot or achieved effect is claimed. Numerical thresholds are proposed design choices, not evidence-based predictions. Establish baseline and feasibility with participants before setting final targets.

## Evidence and existing work

Two randomized studies of the US Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) show why the whole applicant journey matters:

### Application assistance, not just information

Finkelstein and Notowidigdo (2019), [*Take-Up and Targeting: Experimental Evidence from SNAP*](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/aaFinkelstein_Noto_QJE_August_2019%20%281%29.pdf), studied approximately 30,000 Pennsylvania residents aged 60 or over who were enrolled in Medicaid but not SNAP and were likely eligible for SNAP. The intervention took place in 2016.

After nine months, SNAP enrolment was 6% with usual processes, 11% with eligibility information and 18% with information plus an offer of telephone application assistance. Assistance covered forms, supporting documents and follow-up questions.

People induced to enrol by the interventions had higher net incomes and were less sick than those enrolling in the control group. Higher overall uptake therefore did not establish that the people with greatest need were reached equally well.

**Implication for this brief:** test practical help through resolution, and examine who remains excluded. These are enrolment results in a specific population, not measured time savings or evidence of AI effectiveness.

### A bottleneck beyond the form

Giannella, Homonoff, Rino and Somerville (2024), [*Administrative Burden and Procedural Denials: Experimental Evidence from SNAP*](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.20220701), report a field experiment involving approximately 65,000 Los Angeles applicants. Access to flexible interviews initiated by applicants increased approvals by six percentage points.

**Implication for this brief:** identify missed interviews and other procedural barriers before assuming form-filling is the main constraint. A simpler service change may help more than AI assistance.

Both studies evaluated non-AI interventions in US benefit applications. Neither establishes a current local baseline, effects on renewals or billing disputes, applicability elsewhere, or the proposed 50% effort reduction. They inform the comparison and measures; affected-person review and a local test are still needed.

## What is needed to test it

A host with access to the relevant service or setting; people who experience the problem; a domain specialist; consented outcome and cost records; and an independent evaluator. These are requirements, not resources already secured.

Before enrolment, name the sample, comparator, measurement period, full cost and expected uncertainty. Use only necessary data; confirm permissions and provide a practical route to withdraw. For clinical or other high-stakes experiments, obtain appropriate professional and institutional review.

## Who might make it happen

The organisations and services involved in the first experiment are possible hosts or buyers, not committed partners. Public funding, a sustainable business or a cooperative model may all be appropriate. The payment model must leave a demonstrable benefit with the person after fees, travel, equipment, attention and unpaid work.

## What could make life worse

No invented evidence, hidden submission or unauthorised disclosures. Preserve accessible human help and appeal routes.

## Stop or rethink if

The practical benefit fails to reach people; review and setup consume the claimed savings; the approach shifts cost or work onto someone with less power; or important harms increase. Also test whether the stated comparator achieves the same human outcome more simply.

A short-term process gain cannot stand in for a long-term health, income, relationship or safety outcome. Publish failed and abandoned cases as well as successes.

## Making this relevant in different lives

Account for paper-based services, identity-document gaps, local rules, language and people without smartphones.

There is no single average household. Validate with people from different income levels, family structures and access conditions before claiming broad usefulness.

## First contribution wanted

Map one local renewal or billing-dispute process with affected applicants and a service worker, including people who abandoned it or were rejected. Identify where time is spent, where people drop out and whether the obstacle is information, documentation, an interview or the service's own rules.

Ask what improvement would matter and what the measures miss. Use that map to define the baseline and the simplest credible comparison before proposing an AI tool. Share only non-identifying summaries; no interviews or access are claimed here. No impact score is assigned before that work.
