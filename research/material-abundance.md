# When does better production make useful things easier to obtain?

**Supporting research proposal; reviewed 2026-09-21.** This note connects material abundance to [affordable homes](../problems/essentials/PWS-0201.md), [food](../problems/essentials/PWS-0202.md) and [household energy](../problems/essentials/PWS-0203.md). It adds no catalogue entry or validated result. The worked case concerns furniture repair; it does not establish food or construction safety.

## The decision this research should improve

For a household with a broken furniture knob, is a usable replacement cheaper and easier to obtain by buying it, designing and printing it, or using AI to assist the design? A cheap CAD file is not the outcome. The person needs a fitting, durable part without unreasonable effort or expense.

The larger aspiration is reliable clean energy and the ability to shape matter to meet human needs, including repair and recovery rather than continual replacement. A fictional replicator expresses the desired ease of access. It supplies no evidence that arbitrary objects can be made from arbitrary feedstock.

## Capabilities and discontinuities

| Capability or ambition | What is established or proposed | What must still be demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| Digital design and additive fabrication | Existing processes make objects within material and geometry limits; manufacturer guidance describes fit, orientation and iteration requirements. | Useful performance and total cost for this part, process and user. |
| More precise manufacturing | A research direction: control geometry and material properties with fewer errors and less waste. | Repeatable tolerances, throughput, inspection and performance at the required scale. Precision alone does not establish affordability. |
| Atomic positioning | [Eigler and Schweizer (1990)](https://www.nature.com/articles/344524a0) positioned xenon atoms on a nickel surface at 4 K using a scanning tunnelling microscope. | Generality, chemical assembly, scale and operating conditions for a proposed manufacturing application. This experiment is not a household assembler. |
| General-purpose replication | Speculative aspiration for producing many kinds of useful objects on demand. | Feedstock preparation, energy and heat budgets, chemistry, assembly, error correction and verification. No timetable follows from the earlier rows. |

These rows are not a technology-readiness ladder: progress in one does not establish the missing capabilities in another.

## A bounded furniture-repair comparison

**Proposed protocol; no parts fabricated or prices measured.** Use ten distinct replacement cases from ordinary indoor cabinet or drawer knobs with an existing serviceable metal fastener. Exclude appliances, hot surfaces, child-safety devices, load-bearing furniture components and safety-critical functions. A competent maker checks eligibility before work. Record all screened cases and exclusion reasons.

For each case, prepare one shared specification: photographs without identifying surroundings; dimensions in millimeters; existing fastener/thread information; mounting depth and clearance; grip requirements; opening force; surface finish; and acceptable appearance. Inspect the furniture itself so that a jammed drawer is not misclassified as a missing-knob problem. The owner approves the functional requirements without being told which fabrication route is expected to win.

Compare three routes on every case:

1. **Purchase:** find a compatible commercially available knob using a fixed 30-minute search allowance, then record price, shipping, lead time, installation and returns.
2. **Manual CAD:** a maker designs from the same specification, using conventional CAD and documentation.
3. **AI-assisted CAD:** a comparably skilled maker uses the same CAD and documentation plus a recorded AI tool/version, retaining responsibility for geometry and checks.

For the two CAD routes, allow up to 90 minutes active design/correction time and three print attempts per case. These are proposed feasibility budgets, not industry benchmarks. Keep printer, material batch, layer settings and postprocessing resources equivalent; document any justified deviations. Separate designers or conceal the other route's files; balance designer experience and rotate route assignments across cases. Otherwise familiarity with a first design can masquerade as an AI gain.

## What counts as a usable replacement

[Prusa's design guidance](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135) identifies material, orientation, calibration and settings as relevant to fit and advises test iterations. It is manufacturer guidance, not evidence that a particular replacement will work. Use measured tolerances for the selected equipment rather than a universal clearance value.

Before fabrication, the maker and owner record dimensions, allowable deviations, attachment checks, grip criteria and a durability test appropriate to the measured opening force. Preregister the cycle count and load, with a rationale from the intended use; these are unresolved engineering inputs, so physical testing must not begin before they are specified. A proposed pilot uses repeated openings on a fixture, followed by inspection for cracking, looseness and permanent deformation, with guarded loading where a failed part could be ejected.

Assess purchased and fabricated parts against the same acceptance criteria, with the route concealed from the assessor where practical. Preserve failed attempts in the record. Surface appearance alone cannot pass fit or durability. A four-week owner follow-up can identify early failures and usability issues; it cannot establish lifetime durability or product certification.

## Cost and access worksheet

For each route and case, record separate cash cost, active human minutes and elapsed days to an accepted part. Preserve unresolved cases as failures within the budget, not zero-cost replacements.

| Record | Include |
|---|---|
| Inputs | Purchased part/fastener; all filament used, including supports, scrap and rejected attempts; consumables. |
| Equipment | Machine hours multiplied by a disclosed allocation rate covering capital and maintenance; do not charge the whole printer to one part or assume it is free. |
| Energy and tools | Metered electricity including warm-up and failed runs, local tariff, CAD/AI fees allocated consistently. |
| Human work | Searching, measuring, design, checking, monitoring, postprocessing, fitting, returns and correction. |
| Delivery and access | Shipping, travel, installation, accessible assistance and queue time. |

Report cash cost separately from a sensitivity calculation valuing time at **€0, €15 and €30/hour**, explicitly hypothetical rates. For a route with cash cost `C` and active time `h`, comparable economic cost is `C + h × rate`. Report total route expenditure divided by all attempted cases and, separately, by accepted replacements, with the success count. If none succeeds, cost per accepted replacement is undefined, not zero.

For illustration only, a bought part costing €8 delivered plus ten minutes of work totals €13 at €30/hour. A fabricated part with €3 cash/equipment cost and 40 minutes of work totals €23. Fabrication wins on cash but loses on valued time under these assumptions. A lower material bill therefore does not by itself show better access.

Record whether the owner actually obtains and uses the part and who pays for the maker's time. A subsidized pilot must identify the subsidy rather than present it as a scalable market price.

## Energy and recovery are separate constraints

Abundant electricity requires more than generation: storage or other balancing resources, delivery, reliability and access also matter. [EIA's explanation](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/prices-and-factors-affecting-prices.php) includes plant and grid costs and regulation among price factors. Cheap generation is not automatically a low household bill. For this study, record the delivered tariff and interruption losses; larger research should identify which physical or institutional bottleneck it is testing.

Track material discarded, reused and sent to an identified recovery route by mass. Do not label scrap recycled merely because its polymer is theoretically recyclable. A competent materials partner must establish collection, contamination limits and resulting performance before making a recovery claim. More efficient fabrication can still increase total material use if it induces more production; report the boundary of any environmental comparison.

## What the result would justify

Publish all ten cases, including unavailable purchased parts, failed designs and withdrawals. Compare success, cost, human burden and delivery time rather than naming a universal winner. Design budgets may make some routes infeasible; that finding is useful. Stop the affected route for attachment failure, unacceptable grip or damage; redesign if review effort consumes apparent savings.

An AI advantage here would support one CAD-assistance workflow. It would establish neither general-purpose replication nor an assured path to it. Qualified fabrication review, participant feedback, equipment access and a costed host are still required. The first contribution wanted is a maker's critique of the specification and acceptance worksheet, followed by verified inputs for one actual case. No host, funding or achieved benefit is claimed.
